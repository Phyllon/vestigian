'vestigian': patch
---

On a phone, the folded "limes" panel is no longer covered by the key and the map-credit buttons: the sheet gains its own strip along the bottom for them, the key now sits on one line with the credit button, and the map controls and the featured list sit above it. The grey bar at the top of the sheet is a handle you can drag — up to open the panel, down to fold it — and the fold button eases the sheet the same way instead of jumping, while still working for keyboard and screen-reader use. The header menu (and the account and language menus) now stay inside the screen and scroll when they have more entries than fit. The featured-nearby list's scrollbar is wider, high-contrast in light and dark, and stays on screen instead of fading. The map type you last chose (Standard, Satellite, an antiquity sheet…) is remembered on your device and restored on reload, as the light/dark choice already was. When showing the sites brings a new row into the map settings (every site as its own marker), the settings button carries a small dot and its row a "New" tag until you have looked, once, not on every visit. A new Partners page, listed in the menu, presents the organisations that have agreed terms with us in their own words — alphabetically, with what is drawn from them, how to cite them and a badge they may show on their own site. Until a real partner exists it shows only invented examples, under a notice, in development. Vestigian has a mark: a wine-red V whose left arm fades out slice by slice, on the favicon and app icons, on every share card and on the "Seen on Vestigian" badge. The sitemap now lists the inscriptions (about 88,000 pages) and the reading pages it had missed, pages are stable runs of records, and a test keeps it in step with the pages of the app. A Roman watchtower on the limes now says what its guard platform could have seen: a polar diagram with its figures written on it, the view in eight directions with the numbers of the ground behind each (what shuts the view and where ground comes back), the same shapes and distances drawn on the map with a lower and a higher estimate of the tower's height beside them, a switch to see where the tower itself could be seen from, and which neighbouring towers it could have seen, with the ground between them. Each frontier sector gains "Watching the frontier": whether a signal could have passed from tower to tower along the line, where the chain breaks, how much of the line and the country beside it was overlooked by one tower and by three, and the stretches no recorded tower watched, with the links and the ground on the map. Every line tracing gains a profile of the ground along it — climb, fall, the steepest stretch — that you can move along with a finger or the arrow keys, with a dot following it on the map.
